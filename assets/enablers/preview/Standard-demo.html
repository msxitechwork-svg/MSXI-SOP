Step 1: Front Profile and Engine Compartment
The presentation begins directly in front of the vehicle.
This step establishes the vehicle's road presence, exterior aesthetics, and core engineering fundamentals.
The consultant highlights styling cues such as the signature front grille, LED daytime running lights, and active safety hardware like radar sensors or forward collision cameras.
Opening the hood allows the presenter to discuss powertrain choices, performance output, fuel efficiency or electric motor range, and accessible service points.
It is also the ideal moment to emphasize structural safety, pointing out design, engine safety mechanisms in frontal impacts, and under-hood sound insulation that ensures a quiet cabin experience.
Step 2: Driver Profile and Front Quarter
Moving along the driver side profile, the focus shifts to vehicle proportions, ground clearance, and exterior durability.
The consultant draws attention to alloy wheel design, tire specifications, side-impact protection beams engineered into the doors, and aerodynamically sculpted body lines that reduce drag and wind noise.
Key functional elements on this side include heated or power-folding side mirrors with integrated turn signals, blind-spot monitoring indicators, and flush or keyless entry door handles.
This position builds customer confidence in the vehicle's driving dynamics and exterior build quality.
Step 3: Rear Profile and Cargo Space
Continuing to the back of the car, this step focuses on practical utility, rear styling, and reversing safety.
The presenter showcases the rear LED light signatures, aerodynamic roof spoiler, reversing camera placement, and ultrasonic parking sensors.
Opening the trunk or tailgate reveals the cargo capacity, loading lip height, luggage tie-downs, and under-floor storage compartments containing the spare wheel or tire mobility kit.
Demonstrating the flexibility of split-folding rear seats allows the customer to visualize how the four-wheeler accommodates real-world requirements such as grocery runs, luggage for road trips, sports equipment, or bulky family cargo.
Step 4: Rear Passenger Cabin - This should be 4th step
Before placing the primary driver behind the wheel, the consultant opens the rear passenger door and invites the customer or accompanying family members to step inside or sit down.
This step addresses passenger comfort and practical family living.
Key highlights include rear legroom, under-thigh support, headroom, recline angles, flat or low-transmission tunnel floor space, and child safety features.
The presenter also highlights dedicated amenities such as rear air conditioning vents, fold-down center armrests with cup holders, USB charging ports, and rear window sunshades.
Step 5: Passenger Side Exterior - This should be 5th step
Walking around the passenger side completes the exterior walkaround and provides an opportunity to view the vehicle's symmetrical styling and fit-and-finish.
The consultant can highlight the fuel filler lid or charging flap, pointing out ease of access, capless fuelling mechanisms, or fast-charging compatibility.
This position is also well-suited for discussing exterior color options, paint protection, body cladding, roof rail load capacities, and accessory packages, reinforcing the visual appeal from a fresh angle while moving the customer toward the cabin.
Step 6: Driver Cockpit and Controls
The demonstration culminates by placing the prospective buyer directly into the driver seat, with the consultant sitting in the front passenger seat.
The consultant guides the driver through adjusting the seat height, lumbar support, steering wheel rake and reach, and exterior mirrors to establish an ideal ergonomic driving posture.
Once comfortable, the consultant introduces the digital instrument cluster, center touchscreen infotainment system, smartphone connectivity, steering-mounted controls, climate controls, and drive mode selectors.
Setting up while stationary eliminates distractions, builds immediate familiarity with the vehicle's interface, and provides a smooth, confident launch into the test drive.
Essential Guidelines for an Effective Demonstration
Apply the Feature-Advantage-Benefit Method
Avoid listing technical specifications in isolation. Instead, translate each feature into what it actually does (the advantage) and why it matters to the specific customer (the benefit). For example, rather than simply stating that the car has high-strength steel side door beams, explain that this structural reinforcement disperses impact energy away from the cabin during a side collision, ensuring greater protection for their family.
Tailor the Walkaround to Customer Needs
Never deliver an identical, scripted presentation to every buyer. Use insights gathered during the initial qualifying conversation to emphasize what matters most to that individual. If the customer prioritizes fuel economy and daily commuting, focus on engine efficiency, cruise control, and cabin noise reduction. If they are an active family, give additional time to cargo flexibility, rear seat room, and safety ratings.
Encourage Physical Interaction and Touch
A successful demonstration is hands-on rather than passive. Hand the keys or key fob to the customer, invite them to open the tailgate, encourage them to touch interior upholstery and dashboard materials, and have them fold the rear seats flat. Physical interaction fosters an emotional sense of connection and pre-ownership with the vehicle.
Maintain Open Body Language and Proper Pacing
Position yourself at an angle so that you never block the customer's line of sight toward the vehicle. Maintain an open posture, make eye contact, and match the customer's pace instead of rushing them through the sequence. Observe their nonverbal cues; if they linger at the trunk or the infotainment screen, pause and explore those areas in greater detail.
Check for Understanding and Address Questions
Use gentle open-ended questions throughout each step, such as asking how the rear seating comfort compares to their current vehicle or whether the cargo capacity meets their travel habits. This allows you to confirm alignment, clarify doubts naturally, and resolve underlying reservations before the customer ever shifts the vehicle into gear.
